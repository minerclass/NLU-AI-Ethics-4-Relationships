The contradiction I did not resolve

I argue that generative AI can produce the visible artifact of learning without the interpretive work that artifact used to signal.

I wrote the proposal with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Elicit open.

I am not here to tell you I squared that. I am here to show you what I had to write down in order to keep going.

The frame for the next twenty minutes

This dissertation has four different relationships with generative AI, and each one obligates something different.

  1. 01 Subject

    AI is what the study is about: tertiary algorithmicity and pedagogical friction.

    ObligationConceptual precision, and a framework that can be shown to fail.

  2. 02 Assistant

    AI supported the making of the proposal: discovery, organization, revision.

    ObligationDisclosure at the level of the decision, and retained authorship.

  3. 03 Data

    AI output is a bounded, prespecified, nonparticipant comparison source.

    ObligationChain of custody, exactly as with any other evidence.

  4. 04 Infrastructure

    AI helped build the public scholarship surrounding the study.

    ObligationAn enforced boundary between showing and collecting.

Most disclosure statements collapse all four into one sentence. That collapse is the ethics problem.

Relationship one · AI as subject

Pedagogical friction is the intentional preservation of educationally productive resistance.

Tertiary algorithmicity, three pressures

  • Noetic displacement. Cognitive labor is bypassed rather than supported.
  • Rhetorical saturation. Fluent output erodes genuine exchange.
  • Existential abstraction. The claim is severed from the claimant.

The framework, three learner-facing dimensions on one base

  • Noetic friction, the head.
  • Rhetorical friction, the room.
  • Existential friction, the world.
  • Infrastructural friction, the system: the conditioning layer that enables or constrains the other three, not a fourth co-equal dimension.

The equity boundary: friction that is productive for one learner can be exclusionary for another. That distinction is inside the framework, not beside it.

Relationship two · What is actually in the proposal

The AI Use Disclosure sits in the front matter, before Chapter One.

Generative AI and AI-assisted research tools, including ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Elicit, and custom applications, supported brainstorming, organization, literature discovery, source comparison, visualization, and sentence-level revision during proposal development. These tools did not replace the author's responsibility for source selection, verification, interpretation, theoretical development, argumentation, or final written decisions.

Agreement among AI systems was not treated as validation.

Proposal, AI Use Disclosure, controlling copy, July 24, 2026

They share training data. They share failure modes. They converge on fluent consensus. Convergence is not corroboration.

Relationship two · The machinery behind the sentence

A disclosure paragraph is cheap. What makes it a commitment is the apparatus underneath it.

Appendix H, four memo types

  • Reflexive and positionality memo
  • Analytic and code memo
  • Methodological and procedural memo
  • AI-use and process memo: what was supplied, what the tool produced, how the output was checked, and what the researcher decided

Chapter Three, the negative rule

  • AI tools "will not make coding decisions, develop themes, serve as statistical authorities, or determine final interpretations."
  • If approved, they may assist only with organization, comparison, documentation, and quality control.
  • Identifying information is removed before any permitted use, and each use is logged in the audit trail.

The rule exists because the study concerns cognitive bypass. It is a methodological commitment, not a virtue claim.

Relationship two · The transferable part

The AI-use process memo, field by field.

  • Memo ID
  • Date
  • Tool and version
  • Task
  • Material provided
  • Output generated
  • Linked audit entry
  • Chapter or question connection
  • Purpose of AI assistance
  • Boundaries: what the tool was not allowed to do
  • Researcher action: accepted, rejected, revised, verified
  • Verification: which source was checked against
  • Effect on the study
  • Accountability statement

The unit of disclosure is not the tool. It is the decision. A tool list is obsolete by the time you defend. A documented decision boundary survives the version change.

Relationship three · The inversion

The harder question is not whether AI helped me write. It is what happens when AI output is my evidence.

Prespecified before anything is generated

  • System-selection criteria and the final system set
  • The direct-response prompt set
  • The bounded agentic-task protocol
  • The run count
  • Five comparison dimensions: experiential specificity, situated judgment, uncertainty, relational accountability, responsibility

Logged for every run

  • Provider, system, model and version, access date and collection window, interface
  • Exact prompts and task instructions, run and output counts, visible settings, secure filenames
  • For agentic runs, additionally: configuration, tools and permissions, memory and context conditions, network access, human approval points, stopping conditions, action logs, intermediate artifacts, and failures

COREQ and SRQR both predate generative AI. No current qualitative reporting standard tells you how to do this, so I had to write mine where a committee can attack it.

Relationship three · The rules that make it admissible

Five constraints, written into Chapter Three and Appendix D.

  1. No participant data goes to any system.
  2. No agentic run may modify external data, systems, or accounts, or take actions affecting people.
  3. Comparison begins only after themes from human accounts are finalized, so the machine cannot seed the human reading.
  4. AI-generated artifacts are labeled, stored separately, and never interspersed with participant data.
  5. Generated material is never treated as a participant perspective, as evidence of classroom deployment, or as representative of AI broadly.

If AI output is going to function as evidence, it needs a chain of custody like any other evidence.

Relationship four · Live, three stops

The proposal is a document. The scholarship around it is a public ecosystem.

  • 0:30 The canonical front door minerclass.github.io/dissertation-overview/

    One artifact is the entry point and one text is controlling, and they are not the same thing. The site is the canonical entrance; the proposal is the canonical text.
  • 2:00 The immersive companion, cited inside the proposal Overview minerclass.github.io/pedagogical-friction/

    Run the ten-scenario card sort live. Ask the room to call out where the productive and exclusionary line falls.
  • 0:45 The instruments, browser-local by design minerclass.github.io/dissertationquestionsbeta/

    No server, no database, no account system, no third-party transcription. Entries never leave the participant's machine until a file is deliberately downloaded and moved to an IRB-approved secure folder.

Public scholarship before the defense is possible, but only if the boundary is explicit and enforced. The site can show the instrument. It must never collect.

Relationship four · What makes it scholarship rather than a personal website

The governance layer is public too.

In every repository

  • AGENT_LOG.md. Append-only, newest first, never rewritten. Records what was verified and how, and states explicitly what was not verified in each pass.
  • SOURCES_AND_PRIVACY.md. A public statement of what these surfaces may never hold: identifiable participant information, consent records, recordings, transcripts, survey responses, raw exports, private memos, or staff and student information.
  • Dual licensing. MIT for code, CC BY 4.0 for scholarly content.

Persistent and citable

  • Miner (2026a), K-12 teacher AI evidence arc, 2024-2026, Zenodo, doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21152544. Documents the weighting and effective-sample-size approximation behind the descriptive estimates.
  • Miner (2026b), i.e.: inquiry in education, 18(1), Article 4. The peer-reviewed statement of tertiary algorithmicity and unproductive success.

An audit trail that only records successes is marketing. The "not verified this pass" paragraph is the whole ethic in miniature.

The honest part

Four things I have not solved.

  • Disclosure is not justification.

    Documenting that I used a tool does not establish that I should have. My audit trail answers what happened. It does not answer whether it was right.

  • Environmental and labor costs sit outside my design.

    Compute footprint, and the provenance of training data including uncompensated authorship, fall entirely outside my accountability structure. My framework has nothing to say about them.

  • This practice may not be replicable, which makes it an equity problem.

    Building this took paid tool access, a district technology role, and technical fluency most candidates do not have. If AI-assisted public scholarship becomes an expectation, it advantages people positioned like me. That is my own infrastructural friction dimension, aimed back at higher education.

  • My positionality is compromised in a specific way.

    I am a district technology director who evaluates and purchases these systems. I am not a neutral observer of the market I study. Chapter Three names this under power analysis. Naming it does not neutralize it.

Two to read, and one hard floor

Where to take this next.

  • Primary · methodological Jones, K. M. L. (2025). Generative AI in qualitative research and related transparency problems: A novel heuristic for disclosing uses of AI. International Journal of Qualitative Methods, 24. doi.org/10.1177/16094069251404329

    Argues COREQ and SRQR predate generative AI and leave researchers no way to disclose meaningfully. Proposes TROUT-AI: twenty questions across five themes, each with a triggered disclosure action. Put it beside the Appendix H template and ask which fields each one misses.

  • Counterweight · critical Holmes, W., & Zhgenti, S. (2024). Exploring the ethics of generative AI, education, and democracy. Journal of Artificial Intelligence for Sustainable Development.

    Opacity, bias, erosion of teacher agency, and misinformation eroding trust, with a call for critical AI literacy that prioritizes the human dimension. It argues against the posture this session could accidentally model.

  • Normative floor COPE. Authorship and AI tools. publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/authorship-and-ai-tools

    AI tools cannot be listed as authors: they cannot take responsibility for the work, cannot declare conflicts of interest, and cannot hold copyright. This settles the co-author question before the discussion can spiral.

Discussion · about eight minutes

Three questions, escalating.

  1. Where would you draw the line between AI as assistant and AI as co-author? Not where your institution draws it. Where you would.
  2. If two AI systems agree with each other about your literature, what have you actually learned?
  3. Whose dissertation gets better because of these tools, and whose gets worse? Be concrete.
